Sprain or break?

You often can’t tell
just by looking.

Swelling, bruising, and pain can happen with either. An exam—and an X-ray when needed—helps take the guesswork out of it.

Get it evaluated

Don’t brush it off.

  • Pain right over a bone
  • You can’t comfortably bear weight or use the injured area
  • Significant swelling or bruising
  • Pain that persists after an injury

Walk in for an orthopedic evaluation. You don’t need to know what’s wrong first.

Could be either

Movement isn’t proof.

  • You can move it, but it hurts
  • It’s tender when you touch it
  • You can limp on it carefully
  • It looks like “just a sprain”

Being able to move or walk doesn’t rule out a fracture.

Emergency care

Go to the ER now.

  • Bone through the skin or an open wound over a suspected break
  • A limb that looks deformed or out of place
  • Numbness, or a cold, pale, or blue hand or foot
  • Heavy bleeding, major trauma, or a head, neck, or back injury
  • Loss of consciousness or severe, uncontrolled pain

For an emergency, call 911 or go to the nearest emergency room.

General information, not a diagnosis or a complete list of emergency symptoms. If you’re unsure how serious an injury is, seek medical care.

Kids & teens

Small bones.
Special attention.

Kids’ fractures can be easy to mistake for a sprain. A child who’s still using an injured arm may still need an evaluation.

Buckle & greenstick fractures

Growing bones can bend, compress, or crack partway rather than break completely.

Growth-plate concerns

Injuries around a growth plate need careful evaluation and appropriate follow-up.

Do I need an appointment or a referral?
No appointment is needed. Walk in during clinic hours. You can also complete the optional online intake before arriving. You don’t need a referral to visit; check with your insurance plan about any requirements for specialist coverage.
Can I get an X-ray and a cast in the same visit?
On-site X-ray, casting, and splinting are available. Your provider will examine the injury and recommend the right treatment. Some injuries need different care or further imaging.
What if it turns out it isn’t broken?
We also evaluate and treat sprains, strains, and other orthopedic injuries. Your visit is about understanding what’s wrong and making a treatment plan, whether or not a fracture is found.
How much does a visit cost?
The clinic publishes a typical self-pay visit range of $200–$400. Your cost depends on your care and insurance benefits. Call for an estimate and ask what imaging, casting, supplies, and follow-up will cost.
Can you see my child?
Yes. The clinic sees children and adolescents for orthopedic injuries, including suspected fractures and growth-plate concerns. Call if you have questions about your child’s injury before coming in.
What if I need surgery?
If your injury needs surgery, the team will explain why and help coordinate the next steps. Our providers include board-certified orthopedic surgeons.
Where are you, and when are you open?
Find us at 75 E 12300 S, Suite 100, Draper, UT 84020. Hours are Monday–Friday, 9am–8pm; Saturday, 10am–4pm; and Sunday, 10am–2pm. Call to confirm holiday hours.
